# 🎤 Fasting, Nature & the Brain — Study Pack

Soạn từ tài liệu gốc `docs/fasting-nature-brain.md`. Mọi nội dung dưới đây chỉ lấy từ tài liệu đó, không thêm nguồn hay số liệu mới. Nghĩa tiếng Việt chỉ lấy từ mục VOCABULARY của tài liệu; thuật ngữ nào chưa có nghĩa thì ghi "—".

**Core question:** Which habit really changes the brain — intermittent fasting or being in nature?
**Answer:** Both can help, but the proof is not equal. Fasting 2/5 · Nature 4/5 (presenter's own rating, not a published score).

## 1. Concept graph

Mỗi dòng là một cạnh: **A** —[quan hệ]→ **B**. Quan hệ: causes, supports, contrasts, part-of, evidence-for.

### Fasting
- **No food ~12 h** —[causes]→ **glucose runs low**
- **glucose runs low** —[causes]→ **body burns fat → ketones** (backup brain fuel)
- **ketones** —[causes]→ **BDNF ↑** (fertilizer for neurons) — shown in mice
- **metabolic switch** —[part-of]→ **intermittent fasting** (idea from Mark Mattson)
- **gut–brain axis** —[part-of]→ **intermittent fasting** (2nd pathway; Zhao et al., 2025 review, mostly animal studies)
- **NIA 2024 trial** (~40 older adults, 8 weeks) —[evidence-for]→ **fasting & healthy diet both improve cognition** (fasting slightly better on memory/executive function; small study)
- **healthy-adult studies** —[evidence-for]→ **memory results MIXED** (focus dips in first 1–2 weeks)
- **Mattson's book** (popular science, IF advocate) —[supports]→ **mechanism explanation only**, not proof

### Nature
- **Attention Restoration Theory** (Kaplan) —[part-of]→ **two theories** — "soft fascination" → focus recharges
- **Stress Reduction Theory** (Ulrich) —[part-of]→ **two theories** — natural scenes → faster calm after stress
- **Stanford 2015** (Bratman, 90-min walk) —[evidence-for]→ **less rumination** + calmer brain area linked to depression
- **Max Planck 2022** (Sudimac, fMRI, 1-hour forest walk) —[evidence-for]→ **amygdala activity ↓**
- **U. Michigan 2008** (Berman, park vs downtown walk) —[supports]→ **Attention Restoration Theory**
- **White 2019** (~20,000 people, England) —[evidence-for]→ **≥120 min/week → better health & wellbeing** (correlation, not cause; levels off ~200–300 min)

### Limits & answer
- **SMALL · SHORT · LINKS** —[part-of]→ **limitations**
- **correlation** —[contrasts]→ **causation**
- **Cal State LA 2024** (18 people, student study) —[evidence-for]→ **SMALL** (limitation example)
- **Fasting 2/5** (strong in mice, mixed in people) —[contrasts]→ **Nature 4/5** (brain scans + controlled experiments in people)
- **Nature: felt same day, very low risk** —[contrasts]→ **Fasting: weeks–months; some groups must ask a doctor**

### Slide design
- **subitizing** (Kaufman et al., 1949) —[supports]→ **max ~6 objects per slide**
- **working memory ≈ 4±1 chunks** (Cowan, 2001) —[supports]→ **max ~6 objects per slide**
- **7±2** (Miller, 1956) —[contrasts]→ **4±1** (older idea vs newer estimate)
- **redundancy effect** —[causes]→ **keywords + visuals, not full sentences**

<details><summary>glucose — đường glucose</summary>Runs low after ~12 h without food, which starts the metabolic switch.</details>

<details><summary>BDNF — protein nuôi tế bào thần kinh</summary>Brain-derived neurotrophic factor; helps brain cells grow and connect ("fertilizer for neurons"). BDNF ↑ shown in mice.</details>

### C. Being in nature
<details><summary>Attention Restoration Theory — restoration: sự phục hồi</summary>Kaplan: nature = "soft fascination" → focus recharges.</details>
<details><summary>Stress Reduction Theory — —</summary>Ulrich: natural scenes → faster calm after stress.</details>

<details><summary>amygdala — hạch hạnh nhân</summary>Brain's alarm/stress center. Max Planck 2022 (Sudimac): 1-hour forest walk → amygdala activity ↓; busy street = no change.</details>
<details><summary>U. Michigan 2008 (Berman) — —</summary>Park walk vs downtown walk → better attention test scores. Supports Attention Restoration Theory.</details>

### D. Limits, risk & plan
<details><summary>Limitations — —</summary>SMALL (often &lt;100 people), SHORT (hours–weeks, not years), LINKS (correlation ≠ causation).</details>
<details><summary>correlation vs causation — tương quan / nhân quả</summary>Correlation ≠ causation (the LINKS limitation). White 2019 = correlation, not cause.</details>
<details><summary>Who should NOT fast without a doctor? — —</summary>People on diabetes medication, pregnant women, people with an eating-disorder history, under 18.</details>
<details><summary>Speed & risk — —</summary>Nature: felt same day, very low risk. Fasting: weeks–months.</details>
<details><summary>Weekly plan — —</summary>Every night a 12-h overnight fast (dinner 7 pm → breakfast 7 am); 3× a week 40 min outdoors = 120 min; phone in pocket; adjust over a few weeks.</details>

## 3. Quiz (15 câu)

Chạm "Đáp án" để xem.

**1. (Multiple choice)** After about how many hours without food does the metabolic switch start?
A. 4 h · B. 8 h · C. 12 h · D. 24 h
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>C. ~12 hours.</details>

**2. (Fill in the blank)** When glucose runs low, the body burns fat and makes ______, a backup brain fuel.
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>ketones</details>

**3. (Explain in 1 sentence)** What is BDNF?
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>Brain-derived neurotrophic factor — a protein that helps brain cells grow and connect.</details>

**4. (Multiple choice)** In healthy adults, intermittent fasting's effect on memory is:
A. clearly better · B. clearly worse · C. mixed · D. not studied
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>C. Mixed — some improve, others no change; focus dips in the first 1–2 weeks.</details>

**5. (Fill in the blank)** The NIA 2024 trial followed about ______ older adults for ______ weeks.
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>~40 · 8</details>

**6. (Multiple choice)** Which theory says nature gives "soft fascination" so focus recharges?
A. Stress Reduction Theory · B. Attention Restoration Theory · C. Subitizing · D. Redundancy effect
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>B. Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan).</details>

**7. (Explain in 1 sentence)** What is rumination?
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>Replaying negative thoughts.</details>

**8. (Fill in the blank)** In the Stanford 2015 study, a ______-minute nature walk reduced rumination; a city walk caused no change.
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>90</details>

**9. (Multiple choice)** The Max Planck 2022 fMRI study found that a 1-hour forest walk lowered activity in the:
A. hippocampus · B. amygdala · C. cortex · D. gut
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>B. Amygdala — the brain's alarm/stress center.</details>

**10. (Fill in the blank)** White 2019: at least ______ minutes a week in nature → better health and wellbeing; benefits level off around ______ minutes.
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>120 · 200–300</details>

**11. (Explain in 1 sentence)** Why is White 2019 rated LIKELY and not KNOWN?
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>It shows a correlation, not cause.</details>

**12. (Multiple choice)** Which is NOT one of the three limitations (SMALL, SHORT, LINKS)?
A. small samples · B. short studies · C. correlation ≠ causation · D. too expensive
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>D.</details>

**13. (Explain in 1 sentence)** Why does nature get 4/5 but not 5/5?
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>The studies are small, short-term, and some show only correlations.</details>

**14. (Multiple choice)** Who should ask a doctor before fasting?
A. people on diabetes medication · B. pregnant women · C. under 18 · D. all of these
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>D. All of these (also people with an eating-disorder history).</details>

**15. (Fill in the blank)** Max ~6 objects per slide is based on ______ (instant counting) and working memory of about ______ chunks.
<details><summary>Đáp án</summary>subitizing · 4±1</details>
