=== SLIDE DESIGN RULES USED (David JP Phillips, TEDx "How to avoid death by PowerPoint") ===

1. One message per slide.
2. Redundancy effect: don't put full sentences on slides and read them; brain can't read and listen well at the same time → use keywords and visuals.
3. Size: the most important thing is the biggest.
4. Contrast: guide focus with contrast; dark background is easier on the eyes.
5. Max ~6 objects per slide. Science behind it: SUBITIZING (Kaufman et al., 1949) = instantly recognizing ~1–4 (up to ~6) items without counting; working memory ≈ 4±1 chunks (Cowan, 2001), older idea 7±2 (Miller, 1956). The "500% more energy" claim in the video: NEEDS VERIFICATION (no peer-reviewed source found).
Other techniques used: Assertion–Evidence model (headline = a claim, visual = proof), grid system, type scale, hook, open loop, signposting, define-on-first-use, limitations slide, rule of three, callback ending, keyword speaker notes, references as appendix.

=== SLIDE-BY-SLIDE CONTENT + TIMING ===
1 Title (~10s) — name, topic.
2 Quick poll / HOOK (~25s) — "Who skipped breakfast today? Who walked in a park this week?"
3 Big question / OPEN LOOP (~20s) — "Which habit really changes the brain?"
4 Roadmap (~15s) — 1 Fasting → 2 Nature → 3 Limits → 4 Answer + weekly plan.
5 Section: Intermittent Fasting (~5s) — eat in a time window (e.g., 8 h), fast the rest.
6 Metabolic switch (~50s) — after ~12 h without food, glucose runs low → body burns fat → KETONES (backup brain fuel) → BDNF ↑ (fertilizer for neurons) — shown in MICE (LIKELY in animals, UNCERTAIN in humans). Idea from neuroscientist Mark Mattson. 2nd pathway: gut–brain axis (Zhao et al., 2025 review) — mostly animal studies.
7 Healthy adults (~40s) — memory results MIXED: some improve, others no change, focus dips in first 1–2 weeks from hunger. Dementia prevention: animals only.
8 Insulin-resistance trial (~45s) — NIA 2024, ~40 older adults, 8 weeks: both fasting and healthy diet improved cognition; fasting slightly better on memory/executive function. Small study. Section summary: "Fasting is promising — but slow, and mostly proven in mice."
9 Section: Being in Nature (~5s) — parks, forests, beaches.
10 Two theories (~40s) — Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan): nature = "soft fascination" → focus recharges. Stress Reduction Theory (Ulrich): natural scenes → faster calm after stress.
11 Study 1 (~30s) — Stanford 2015 (Bratman): 90-min nature walk → less RUMINATION (replaying negative thoughts) + calmer brain area linked to depression; city walk = no change. KNOWN (short-term).
12 Study 2 (~30s) — Max Planck, Berlin 2022 (Sudimac): fMRI, 1-hour forest walk → AMYGDALA (brain's alarm/stress center) activity ↓; busy street = no change. KNOWN (short-term).
13 Study 3 (~25s) — U. Michigan 2008 (Berman): park walk vs downtown walk → better attention test scores. Supports Attention Restoration Theory.
14 Weekly dose (~40s) — White 2019: ~20,000 people in England; ≥120 min/week in nature → better health & wellbeing; levels off ~200–300 min; one long or several short visits both count. Correlation, not cause (LIKELY).
15 Limitations (~40s) — SMALL (often <100 people; e.g., Cal State LA 2024 student study, 18 people), SHORT (hours–weeks, not years), LINKS (correlation ≠ causation).
16 The answer (~30s) — Fasting 2/5, Nature 4/5.
17 Speed & risk (~25s) — Nature: felt same day, very low risk. Fasting: weeks–months; NOT for people on diabetes medication, pregnant women, eating-disorder history, under 18 → ask a doctor.
18 Weekly plan (~40s) — every night 12-h overnight fast (dinner 7 pm → breakfast 7 am); 3× a week 40 min outdoors = 120 min; phone in pocket; adjust over a few weeks (Mattson).
19 Close (~25s) — CALLBACK: "If you skipped breakfast today… good. Now go for a walk." RULE OF THREE: "Fast smart. Walk green. Repeat." "Any questions?"
20–21 Appendix: references.

=== Q&A PREP ===

* Is fasting safe? → 12-h overnight is a gentle start for healthy adults; certain groups must ask a doctor.
* Does a city park count? → Yes; studies counted parks/green spaces.
* Why nature 4/5 not 5? → small, short-term, some only correlations.
* What is BDNF? → brain-derived neurotrophic factor; helps brain cells grow and connect.
* Do both together? → yes, e.g., morning park walk before breakfast; start slowly.
